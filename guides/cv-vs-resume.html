CV vs Resume: What's the Difference, and Which Do You Need?

Updated November 2026 · 5 minute read

If you've seen job ads ask for a "CV" and others ask for a "resume" and wondered whether you need two different documents, the short answer is: it depends entirely on where you're applying, not what the document actually contains.

In the United States and Canada

"Resume" is the short (usually 1-2 page) document you send for almost any job — it lists your work experience, education, and skills, tailored to the specific role. "CV" (curriculum vitae) means something different and much longer: a comprehensive academic record used mainly for university faculty positions, research roles, medical/scientific careers, and grant or fellowship applications. A US employer asking for a "CV" for a standard corporate job is rare and usually means they just want your resume.

In the UK, Ireland, Europe, the Gulf, and most of the world

"CV" is simply the everyday word for the document Americans call a resume. There's no separate "resume" vs "long academic CV" distinction in everyday use — job seekers in London, Dublin, Berlin, Dubai, or Karachi applying for a standard job send a "CV," and it's expected to be concise (often 1-2 pages, sometimes up to 2-3 in parts of Europe), not the exhaustive academic document the US term implies.

So which one do you need?

For almost everyone reading this: you need the short, tailored, 1-2 page version — call it a resume if you're applying in the US or Canada, and a CV everywhere else. The content and structure are nearly identical: contact details, a brief summary, work experience with achievements, education, and skills.

The exception is genuinely academic, medical, or research positions worldwide — those do call for the longer, exhaustive CV format regardless of country, listing publications, grants, conference presentations, and full academic history.

What this means practically

  • Applying for a standard job in the US? Use "Resume" as the file name and build the short format.
  • Applying anywhere else for a standard job? Use "CV" as the file name — same short format, different label.
  • Applying for an academic, research, or medical/scientific post anywhere? You likely need the longer academic CV format, which is a different document from what this guide covers.
