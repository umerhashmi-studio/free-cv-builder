ATS-Friendly Resume Tips: Why Your CV Gets Rejected by Software

Updated November 2026 · 6 minute read

Most large employers — and plenty of mid-size ones — use an Applicant Tracking System (ATS) such as Workday, Taleo, iCIMS, Greenhouse, or SuccessFactors to collect and pre-screen applications before a recruiter ever opens them. If your resume is formatted in a way the ATS can't parse correctly, your actual experience may never reach a human, no matter how qualified you are.

What an ATS actually does

An ATS extracts text from your uploaded file and sorts it into fields: name, contact info, work history, education, skills. Some systems also rank candidates by keyword match against the job description. The problem isn't that ATS software is picky for no reason — it's that many visually appealing resume designs rely on layout tricks that text-extraction simply can't handle reliably.

Formatting mistakes that commonly break parsing

  • Multi-column layouts. Two-column designs (common in Canva templates) often get read left-to-right across both columns instead of down one column at a time, scrambling your work history and dates.
  • Text inside images, icons, or text boxes. Some ATS engines can't extract text that isn't in the main document flow — contact info placed inside a graphic sidebar can simply vanish.
  • Tables used for layout. Similar issue to columns — table-based layouts can scramble reading order.
  • Unusual fonts or heavy symbols. Decorative fonts and excessive icons/emoji can confuse text extraction or simply look unprofessional once parsed back to plain text.
  • Headers/footers for key info. Some systems skip header/footer regions entirely — don't put your name or contact details only there.
  • Photos on roles where they're not expected. Not a parsing issue so much as a norms issue — in the US and most ATS-heavy corporate pipelines, a photo is unnecessary at best and can trigger unconscious bias concerns for the employer at worst.

What actually works

  • A single-column layout, read top to bottom in one continuous flow.
  • Standard section headings: "Work Experience," "Education," "Skills" — not creative renamings the ATS keyword-matcher won't recognize.
  • Dates written out clearly and consistently (e.g. "Jan 2022 – Present").
  • Keywords from the actual job posting worked naturally into your bullet points — ATS ranking is often literally keyword-matching against the posting.
  • A plain, real PDF export (not a scanned image of your resume, and not a design tool's "export as image" option).

A quick way to self-check

Copy the text out of your exported PDF and paste it into a plain text editor. If your name, dates, and section headings all come out in the right order and nothing is missing, that's a good sign the file will parse the same way in an ATS. If it's scrambled, your layout likely will be too.
