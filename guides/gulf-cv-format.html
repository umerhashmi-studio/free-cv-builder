Gulf CV Format: How to Write a CV for the UAE, Saudi Arabia and Qatar

Updated November 2026 · 7 minute read

Employers in the Gulf (the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait, Oman and Bahrain) hire from all over the world, and hiring there depends heavily on visas and sponsorship. A Gulf CV therefore carries personal details that would be left off in the US or UK, because the recruiter needs them to know whether and how quickly you can be hired.

The short version

  • A professional photo is common and often expected.
  • State your nationality, current location and visa status near the top.
  • Two pages is typical. Three is acceptable for senior or technical roles.
  • Give a phone number with country code that also works on WhatsApp.

Personal details recruiters look for

  • Nationality. Visa rules, quotas and salary bands can depend on it.
  • Current location. "Dubai, UAE" or "Lahore, Pakistan". Candidates already in the country can interview and start sooner.
  • Visa status. For example: visit visa valid until a given month, employment visa (transferable), family or spouse visa, or "requires sponsorship".
  • Availability. "Immediate" or your notice period.
  • Languages. English level, and Arabic if you have any. Arabic is a real advantage in customer-facing and government-related roles.
  • Driving licence. A valid licence from a Gulf country matters for sales, site and field jobs.

Date of birth and marital status are still widely included and some employers ask for them, because they affect family visa sponsorship and accommodation. They are optional unless the advert requests them.

Do not put your passport number, national ID number or a passport copy on a CV that you send out widely. Share those only once a real employer is processing an offer.

The photo

Use a recent passport-style photo: plain light background, business dress, face clearly visible. No selfies, cropped group photos or filters.

Section order

  1. Header. Name, target job title, photo, phone and WhatsApp, email, location.
  2. Personal details. Nationality, visa status, availability, languages.
  3. Professional summary. Three lines, including your total years of experience and any Gulf experience.
  4. Work experience. Most recent first. Name the country for each job.
  5. Education. Degree, university, country, year.
  6. Certifications, licences and skills.

Make Gulf experience easy to find

Many adverts ask for "GCC experience". If you have worked in the region, say so in the summary ("8 years in construction project control, 5 of them in Saudi Arabia") and name the city and country beside each role. If you have none, lead with what transfers: international clients, recognised certifications, and experience with the same equipment, software or standards.

Documents to have ready

Work visa processing in the Gulf commonly requires your degree certificate to be attested, first by the authorities in the country that issued it and then by the embassy of the country you are moving to. Requirements differ by country and profession, and regulated professions such as healthcare, engineering and teaching often need a local licence or registration as well. Confirm the current rules with your employer. Starting attestation early can remove weeks from your joining date.

Applying through company portals

Airlines, banks, energy companies and government-linked groups in the region take applications through online systems that extract the text from your CV. Keep the layout simple so the text parses cleanly, and fill in every field of the online form even when it repeats your CV. Our ATS tips explain what trips these systems up.

Avoiding fake job offers

Recruitment fraud aimed at Gulf job seekers is common. Treat these as warning signs:

  • Anyone asking you to pay for a visa, a "processing fee", a medical or a job offer. Legitimate employers bear recruitment and visa costs.
  • An offer with no interview.
  • Emails from free webmail addresses claiming to be a large company.
  • Pressure to send passport copies or money urgently.

Check that the vacancy exists on the company's own careers page, and contact the company through the details on its official website if you are unsure.
