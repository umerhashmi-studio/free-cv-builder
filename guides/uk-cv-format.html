UK CV Format: Layout, Length and What Employers Expect

Updated November 2026 · 6 minute read

In the UK the document is called a CV, it runs to two pages, and it opens with a short personal profile. It is closer to a US resume than to the long, detail-heavy CVs common in parts of Europe, the Gulf and South Asia.

The short version

  • Two A4 pages is the norm. One page is fine for a first job.
  • No photo, date of birth, marital status or nationality.
  • Start with a three or four line personal profile.
  • Reverse-chronological order and British spelling throughout.

Length

Two pages is what most UK recruiters expect from anyone with a few years of experience. School leavers and new graduates often fit on one. Going to three is accepted only in fields where the detail matters, such as academia, medicine and some contracting roles with long project lists.

What to leave off

The Equality Act 2010 protects candidates from discrimination on grounds including age, race, religion, sex and marital status, so UK employers do not want those details and many prefer not to see them. Leave out your photo, date of birth, marital status, religion and nationality. Your town or city and postcode area are enough for an address.

Employers must check your right to work in the UK before you start, but that happens with documents at offer stage. If your status is a selling point, for example you already hold a visa that lets you work without sponsorship, one line in your profile is reasonable.

Section order

  1. Contact details. Name, town, phone, email, LinkedIn.
  2. Personal profile. Three or four lines: who you are professionally, your main strengths, and the kind of role you want.
  3. Employment history. Most recent first, with job title, employer, dates and achievement-led bullets.
  4. Education and qualifications. Degree with classification (for example 2:1), institution and year. Recent graduates can add A-levels; GCSEs can be summarised in one line.
  5. Skills. Software, languages, licences and professional certifications.
  6. Interests. Optional. Include only if they say something specific about you.

The personal profile

This paragraph is the most distinctively British part of the document, and it is read first. Keep it concrete:

  • Weak: "Hard-working team player seeking a challenging role."
  • Better: "Management accountant with five years in retail finance, ACCA qualified, experienced in month-end reporting for a 40-store business. Looking for a finance business partner role."

Gaps, references and spelling

UK recruiters notice unexplained gaps, so account for them in a line: "Career break, caring for a family member, 2023 to 2024" is enough. You do not need to list referees. "References available on request" is optional and many people now drop it to save space. Use British spelling ("organised", "programme", "centre") and day-before-month dates.

Online applications

Larger UK employers and most recruitment agencies load CVs into applicant tracking systems or searchable databases. A single-column CV with plain headings is the safest format for both. Send a PDF unless the advert asks for Word. More on this in our ATS tips.

Applying from abroad

If your experience is from outside the UK, add a few words of context that a UK reader would not have: the size of the company, the sector, and a UK equivalent for your qualification if there is a recognised one. Write your phone number with its country code.
