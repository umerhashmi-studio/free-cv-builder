US Resume Format: Length, Sections and What to Leave Off

Updated November 2026 · 6 minute read

American employers call the document a resume, and they expect it to be short, impersonal and built around results. If you learned to write a CV in the UK, Europe, the Gulf or South Asia, most of what changes is what you remove.

The short version

  • One page if you have under about ten years of experience, two pages at most beyond that.
  • No photo, date of birth, marital status, nationality or religion.
  • Reverse-chronological order, with bullet points that state results.
  • US spelling, US date order (month before day) and US Letter paper.

What to leave off

US anti-discrimination law makes employers wary of anything that reveals age, race, religion, national origin or family status. A resume that carries those details looks unfamiliar at best, and some companies discard resumes with photos so that nobody can later claim the photo influenced the decision. Leave out:

  • Photo
  • Date of birth or age
  • Marital status, number of children, religion
  • Nationality, passport or ID numbers
  • Full street address. City and state are enough.
  • "References available on request." It is assumed.

If you need visa sponsorship, the resume is usually not the place to say so. Application forms ask the question directly, and you should answer it honestly there.

Section order

  1. Header. Name, city and state, phone, email, LinkedIn URL.
  2. Summary. Two or three lines on what you do and your strongest result. Optional, but useful when you are changing fields.
  3. Work experience. Most recent job first. Job title, employer, location, month and year.
  4. Education. Degree, institution, graduation year. Recent graduates put this above experience.
  5. Skills. Tools, languages and certifications that match the job ad.

Write bullets as results

US recruiters skim for evidence, so each bullet should start with a verb and end with an outcome, with a number where you honestly have one.

  • Weak: "Responsible for the customer support team."
  • Better: "Led a 6-person support team and cut average response time from 9 hours to 2."

Three to five bullets per job is typical. Older jobs can shrink to one or two.

Paper size, dates and spelling

The US uses Letter paper (8.5 x 11 inches), which is slightly wider and shorter than A4. A PDF made on A4 still opens normally on any screen, and most applications never get printed, but if you expect a paper copy to be handed around, choose "fit to page" when printing. Write dates as "March 2024" or "03/2024" and use American spelling: "organized", "center", "program".

Applicant tracking systems

Most mid-size and large US employers receive applications through an applicant tracking system (ATS) that extracts the text of your resume into a database. Single-column layouts with standard headings such as "Work Experience" and "Education" parse most reliably. Tables, text boxes, icons and two-column designs are where parsing errors usually come from. Our ATS resume tips cover this in detail.

Resume or CV?

In the US, "CV" means a long academic record used for faculty, research and medical posts. For any other job, send a resume even if you call the same document a CV at home. See CV vs resume for the full distinction.
